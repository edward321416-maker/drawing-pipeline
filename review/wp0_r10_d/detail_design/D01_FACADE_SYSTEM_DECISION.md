## Options
A (recommended preliminary concept): thermally broken aluminum unitized glazing on repetitive ordinary facade; external fins supported by a separately engineered secondary support/load path to verified primary structure. Valley glazing without invented fins; consider localized stick-built transition only where verified geometry calls for it. Advantages: repetitive floor modules, factory assembly, separate fin loading, maintainability; risk: bracket access, differential movement, thermal bridges and penetration detailing; exact primary support zone and installation tolerances unverified.
B: stick-built mullion-transom glazing with independent external fin support. Advantage: greater field adjustment for irregular geometry; disadvantage: site assembly/quality control and high-rise access demands; same independent fin-support validation required.
C: unitized facade with fins on purpose-engineered facade framing, integral load transfer to primary structure. Advantage: integrated prefabrication and fewer separate support zones where suitable; downside: fin eccentricity/wind/thermal movements and glass/air-water joints coupled with frame design; cannot simply attach fins to unverified mullions or caps. Requires manufacturer engineering and validated geometry.
All options preserve existing visible silhouette, fin pattern and location, mullion rhythm, floor levels and garden opening. No cost, load or performance claim. No product selected, no change to approved geometry. Material concept for A: aluminum glazing frame with insulating glazing and noncombustible metal fins, finish matching existing appearance; actual alloy, glass composition, fire rating, coating, fixing and profile dimensions pending design. Structural supports and facade anchors require structural/facade engineering. Do not draw invented final anchor sizes.
